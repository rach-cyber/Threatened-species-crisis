Australia's Silent Extinction

Australia is home to some of the most unique wildlife on Earth — found nowhere else. Yet today, more than 2,000 species are threatened with extinction, and populations have declined by an average of 73% since 1985. This is the story of a crisis unfolding quietly, species by species, state by state.

Act 1 — The Scale

How bad is it?

Australia leads the world in mammal extinctions

Since European settlement in 1788, Australia has lost more mammal species than any other country on Earth. The threatened species list has grown 54% in just 25 years — and the most severe category has grown 33-fold.

Act 4 — The Trend

Is it getting worse?

Populations have been collapsing for four decades

Since 1985, monitored populations of threatened birds, mammals and plants have declined sharply and consistently. The 2017–2019 drought accelerated losses across the board. There is no sign of recovery at a national scale.

Act 5 — The Gap

Where are we not looking?

Most of Australia's interior goes unwatched

We can only measure what we monitor. Thousands of survey sites track threatened species across Australia — but they cluster heavily along the eastern coast. Vast stretches of the interior have almost no monitoring at all.
